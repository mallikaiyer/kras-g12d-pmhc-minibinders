### Position 6 reorients on receptor binding

Distances to HLA Arg114 CZ — the atom the Asp carboxylate pairs with when buried. Cβ is observed where a real side chain exists and reconstructed from the backbone frame otherwise (validated against an observed Cβ to 0.09 Å).

| state | residue | TCR | Cα→ref | Cβ→ref | Cβ−Cα | points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7OW3 C | Gly6 | no | 10.94 | 11.87 | **+0.93** | out of groove |
| 7OW3 L | Gly6 | no | 10.51 | 11.39 | **+0.88** | out of groove |
| 7OW5 C | Gly6 | **yes** | 7.41 | 6.45 | **−0.96** | into groove |
| 7OW6 C | Asp6 | yes | 6.42 | 5.72 | **−0.70** | into groove |
| 7PB2 C / H | Asp6 | yes | 6.50 / 6.34 | 5.60 / 5.65 | −0.90 / −0.69 | into groove |

Two things change between free and bound: the backbone drops ~4.3 Å, and the sign of (Cβ−Cα) inverts.

**7OW5 is the control that makes this interpretable.** It is the same glycine as 7OW3 and the same reconstruction method, differing only in whether a receptor is bound — so the comparison isolates the conformational change from the identity of the residue. The flip is a property of receptor engagement, not of the mutation.

**What this table does and does not establish.** Cβ is fully determined by φ/ψ — Cα is a stereocentre with no rotational freedom once N, Cα and C are fixed — so the orientation above is determinate. But χ1 and χ2 are free, so the table *bounds* where the carboxylate can sit without fixing its direction. Reachability is settled by the rotamer scan below, not by this table.

### Every viable Asp rotamer on the open backbone is exposed

`scripts/02_asp_rotamer_scan.py` builds an aspartate at every (χ1, χ2) on a 10°×15° grid — 432 rotamers per copy, χ2 over 180° because the carboxylate is planar and its oxygens equivalent — and scores each for steric viability, exposure, and distance to Arg114 CZ.

| | 7OW3 chain C | 7OW3 chain L | closed state (7OW6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| clash-free rotamers | **432/432** | **278/432** | — |
| side-chain SASA | 47–103 Å² | 38–96 Å² | **7.4 Å²** |
| carboxylate → Arg114 CZ | 10.1–13.9 Å | 9.8–13.5 Å | **3.64 Å** |

The clash filter is doing real work: chain L's closest approach bottoms out at 1.68 Å across the grid, so genuine collisions were rejected. Chain C bottoms out at 2.96 Å — that backbone has unobstructed side-chain space.

The rotamer freedom is large but **the outcome is not ambiguous**: every sterically viable rotamer leaves the carboxylate solvent-exposed at ≥38 Å², five to fourteen times the buried closed-state value, and none brings it within 9.8 Å of Arg114. An Asp on the open backbone can point many ways; none of them reach the groove.

*Caveats.* The virtual Cβ is not inserted into the structure, so it does not occlude its own carboxylate — exposure is a slight over-estimate. Intra-residue contacts at position 6 are excluded from clash detection, so a strained χ1 is not penalised by sterics alone; relaxation handles that. And this is a **wild-type** backbone: 7OW4 has no density at 5–6 in any copy, so the real G12D free peptide is more mobile than any single conformer implies.

### Backbone compatibility

φ/ψ at position 6: 7OW3 C = −69.6°/+48.3°, 7OW3 L = −86.4°/+41.7°, 7OW6 = −37.2°/+117.7°. Both free copies have **negative φ**, so the open backbone is not glycine-exclusive (Gly-only territory is overwhelmingly positive φ) and an Asp can occupy it. The free copies sit in the bridge region between αR and β — allowed but not most-favoured, so relaxation is warranted. The *observed* Asp6 in 7OW6 is also in a sparsely populated φ/ψ combination, so marginal dihedrals at position 6 are a property of this peptide in this groove, not an artefact of a model.

### The TCR's discrimination is not contact-mediated

Minimum distance from the Asp6 carboxylate (CG/OD1/OD2) to the residues carrying the largest per-residue binding-energy differences in 7OW6:

| TCR residue | ΔΔG | → carboxylate | | HLA residue | → carboxylate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R50α | 2.0 kcal/mol | **13.51 Å** | | Arg114 | **2.95 Å** |
| K70α | 0.88 kcal/mol | **15.89 Å** | | Gln70 | **3.08 Å** |
| K94β | — | 13.18 Å | | Gln155 | 4.69 Å |
| Q99β | — | 9.20 Å | | Asp116 | 5.01 Å |
| H100β | — | 7.02 Å | | Lys146 | 9.99 Å |

The paper's clause "directly positioned above the mutant residue" refers to **the CDR3β loop**, not to these residues individually; K70α is explicitly described as "further from the mutation site". The mechanism is long-range electrostatic complementarity to a more negative surface potential, not a hydrogen-bond network. The carboxylate's actual partners are all on the HLA.

*Note:* this table is defined for 7OW6 only. 7PB2 carries the parent JDI TCR, whose chain assignment and numbering do not correspond — applying the decomposition to it reports distances for unrelated residues, and the code refuses to.

### Affinity calibration (Poole et al. Tables 1–2)

The parent JDI TCR binds G12D at **63 µM** with **no detectable binding** to wild-type, and is clean against G12C, ERAS, RRAS, MRAS, DIRAS2 and RAP1A. As affinity was matured, wild-type binding *appeared*: NB → 25.8 µM → 3.0 µM → 425 nM. **Micromolar affinity is the target, not nanomolar** — over-optimising affinity degrades the objective ranked first.

# Route A (primary) — conformational selection on the open state

## Rationale

Design a binder that engages an Asp6 projecting out of the groove, on the experimentally observed **open** backbone of the free complex.

The mechanistic claim is conformational selection, not induced fit: the open state need only be *populated* in the free ensemble, not dominant. The penalty on apparent affinity is −RT ln *p* — about 1.4 kcal/mol at 10% population, 2.7 at 1%. A binder that engages the open state shifts the population toward it.

Four things support this:

1. **The disorder in 7OW4 is evidence for the premise.** No density at residues 5–6 in any of four copies means the free G12D peptide centre is genuinely sampling multiple conformations. Conformational selection requires a populated alternative state; disorder is what that looks like in a diffraction experiment.
2. **The open state is an experimental observation, not a model.** Two independent copies of 7OW3 agree to within 0.5 Å, and the 7OW5 control shows the open/closed flip is driven by receptor binding rather than by the mutation.
3. **Reachability is measured, not inferred.** Every clash-free Asp rotamer on that backbone is solvent-exposed at ≥38 Å².
4. **The discriminator is Asp-versus-nothing, and negative design comes built in.** A pocket presenting a cation and backbone donors sized for a carboxylate is, against wild-type, a desolvated unsatisfied cation — actively destabilising, not merely neutral. The same feature serves objectives 1 and 3. Objective 2 follows: apo HLA has an empty groove and nothing for the reader to grab.

**Known headwinds.** The groove rewards burial — only the G12D peptide forms a network with the HLA F-pocket in the bound state, so the closed conformation is *more* stabilised for the mutant than for WT. And the entropy is paid twice: ordering a mobile region, in a non-preferred conformation. The TCR paid TΔS° = −8.13 kcal/mol across ~990 Å² buried; a 60–70 residue miniprotein buries ~800–900 Å², so there is margin but little.

**Expected outcome:** a binder with a real mechanism and a genuine chance at objective 1. Not a high-probability binder.

**03 — Build the design targets.** 7OW3 copies **C** (HLA chain A) and **L** (chain J), each as peptide + heavy chain + its paired β2m. Mutate Gly6→Asp. Build both copies rather than choosing: chain L has the more comfortable φ and lower B-factors, chain C has unobstructed rotamer space, neither dominates, and they are independent observations of the same conformation. Take 2–3 distinct χ1 wells per copy from the stage-02 scan, preferring high-SASA clash-free rotamers in canonical wells.

**04 — Protonate, relax, test persistence.** PDBFixer for missing atoms and hydrogens at pH 7.4; restrained minimisation in OpenMM (amber14-all + implicit GBn2), heavy-atom restraints on HLA and β2m, peptide free. Confirm position-6 φ/ψ has not left the allowed region and χ1/χ2 remain canonical. Then unrestrained MD of the pMHC alone, implicit solvent, 3 × 5 ns, tracking the position-6 Cβ→Arg114 CZ distance and Asp6 side-chain SASA. Minimisation alone cannot answer whether the state is populated — it always converges to something strain-free, and strain-free ≠ populated.

**05 — Generate backbones.** RFdiffusion on Colab, binder mode, target = HLA α1/α2 + peptide. Hotspots on peptide residues 4–7 plus the Asp. `noise_scale_ca=0.5`, `noise_scale_frame=0.5`. Checkpoint to Drive.

**06 — Design sequences.** ProteinMPNN, `--sampling_temp 0.1 --omit_AAs C`, 8 sequences per backbone.

**07 — Mechanism audit (the primary filter).** Confirm the designed reader group is within 3.2 Å of the Asp6 carboxylate and buried; then thread the WT peptide into the same model and confirm the reader is left unsatisfied and desolvated. A design that passes has a mechanism; one that fails has a correlation. Cheap, local, and it tests the thing being claimed. Both `biotite.structure.sasa` and freesasa are available, so burial can be cross-checked against an independent implementation.

**08–11** as listed: PRODIGY on G12D and WT-threaded complexes ranked by difference; co-fold finalists against all three targets ranked by ipSAE(G12D) − max(ipSAE(WT), ipSAE(apo)); Foldseek/MMseqs2 novelty; pLDDT, ipSAE and mean Kyte–Doolittle hydropathy.

**Route-specific risk — read before trusting stage 09.** Co-folding models are trained on the PDB, where this peptide's centre is either absent or closed. They may refuse to reproduce the open state and return poor ipSAE for reasons unrelated to design quality. The selection prompt computes ipSAE itself at fixed settings, so the screening metric is structurally biased against this mechanism. Mitigation: state it explicitly in the methods; rely on stage 07 as primary evidence; place the best-argued design in row 1, which the prompt protects (*"a failed screen … never removes a participant's ranked top pick"*).

# Route B (alternative) — closed state, indirect electrostatics

Run only if stage 04 says the open state is not populated.

## Rationale

Design against 7OW6 with the TCR stripped, reproducing Poole's validated mechanism: a paratope electrostatically complementary to the more negative potential the buried Asp6 projects upward.

**What recommends it:** the mechanism is experimentally demonstrated on this exact target, with >4000× discrimination and MMPBSA agreement (12.6 vs 10.8 kcal/mol experimental). The HLA α1/α2 surface is invariant across all five structures, so a binder reading mostly that surface plus the peptide backbone is far more likely to fold and bind.

**What it costs:**

- 7OW6's peptide conformation exists *because a TCR is bound*. This route does not escape the conformational-selection argument; it makes the same bet on a different member of the ensemble.
- The discriminating signal acts from 13–16 Å, now measured. That is not deliberately designable with free tools, and not verifiable with them either.
- The invariant HLA surface is identical in all three assay targets, so the default outcome is a binder that hits all of them — failing objectives 1 and 2.

So this route plays for a different prize: **objective-3 honourable mention and getting tested**, not for the win. A legitimate bet given the goal hierarchy, but not a safer path to the same outcome.

## Steps

1. 7OW6, keep chains A (HLA), B (β2m), C (peptide with real Asp6); delete D and E (TCR). Light restrained minimisation only — the coordinates are experimental.
2. RFdiffusion hotspotted over peptide residues 4–7 and the flanking groove rim (HLA ~65–72, ~150–158). **Do not hotspot D6** — it is 2.95 Å from Arg114 and unreachable; aiming at it produces clashing designs.
3. ProteinMPNN, then filter for net-positive formal charge among binder residues within ~12 Å of peptide residue 6 — the paper's mechanism as a selectable property.
4. PDB2PQR + APBS on finalists; confirm a positive potential patch facing the peptide centre. Same analysis as Poole et al. Fig. 3e,f (±2 eV), cheap on CPU. *Not currently in the environment — add only if switching.*
5. Affinity proxy and counter-screen as Route A, noting the PRODIGY caveat below.
6. Novelty, screens, package as Route A.

## Decision rule

Set before seeing the result:

- **Open state persists over a few ns** (Cβ→Arg114 CZ stays > ~9 Å, Asp6 SASA stays > ~20 Å²) → Route A, full allocation.
- **Position 6 collapses into the groove within ~1 ns** → population penalty likely 3–4+ kcal/mol, Route A will bind nothing. Switch to Route B.
- **Mixed / samples both** → Route A primary, 2–3 Route B designs in rows 8–10 as a hedge.

Keep Route B *trailing*, not parallel — abandonable at any point without having cost the primary.

## Affinity prediction — PRODIGY

Xue et al., *Bioinformatics* 2016, [10.1093/bioinformatics/btw514](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btw514). Installed as `prodigy-prot`.

PRODIGY predicts ΔG from counts of interfacial contacts by residue class plus non-interacting surface. Two consequences for the methods document:

- **It suits Route A.** Replacing Asp6 with Gly removes side-chain contacts outright, which PRODIGY registers directly.
- **It is near-blind to Route B's discriminator.** With no contact to position 6, the contact map barely changes between G12D and WT, so predicted ΔΔG sits near zero whether or not the design works. For Route B the APBS check is the on-mechanism evidence and PRODIGY only bounds overall affinity.

Trained on natural complexes with ~1.5 kcal/mol error, so use it for **ranking within a congeneric series**, never as an absolute ΔG.
