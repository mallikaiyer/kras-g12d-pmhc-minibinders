### Position 6 reorients on receptor binding

Distances to HLA Arg114 CZ — the atom the Asp carboxylate pairs with when buried. Cβ is observed where a real side chain exists and reconstructed from the backbone frame otherwise (validated against an observed Cβ to 0.09 Å).

| state | residue | TCR | Cα→ref | Cβ→ref | Cβ−Cα | points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7OW3 C | Gly6 | no | 10.94 | 11.87 | **+0.93** | out of groove |
| 7OW3 L | Gly6 | no | 10.51 | 11.39 | **+0.88** | out of groove |
| 7OW5 C | Gly6 | **yes** | 7.41 | 6.45 | **−0.96** | into groove |
| 7OW6 C | Asp6 | yes | 6.42 | 5.72 | **−0.70** | into groove |
| 7PB2 C / H | Asp6 | yes | 6.50 / 6.34 | 5.60 / 5.65 | −0.90 / −0.69 | into groove |

Two things change between free and bound: the backbone drops ~4.3 Å, and the sign of (Cβ−Cα) inverts.

**7OW5 is the control that makes this interpretable.** It is the same glycine as 7OW3 and the same reconstruction method, differing only in whether a receptor is bound — so the comparison isolates the conformational change from the identity of the residue. The flip is a property of receptor engagement, not of the mutation.

**What this table does and does not establish.** Cβ is fully determined by φ/ψ — Cα is a stereocentre with no rotational freedom once N, Cα and C are fixed — so the orientation above is determinate. But χ1 and χ2 are free, so the table *bounds* where the carboxylate can sit without fixing its direction. Reachability is settled by the rotamer scan below, not by this table.

### Every viable Asp rotamer on the open backbone is exposed

`scripts/02_asp_rotamer_scan.py` builds an aspartate at every (χ1, χ2) on a 10°×15° grid — 432 rotamers per copy, χ2 over 180° because the carboxylate is planar and its oxygens equivalent — and scores each for steric viability, exposure, and distance to Arg114 CZ.

| | 7OW3 chain C | 7OW3 chain L | closed state (7OW6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| clash-free rotamers | **432/432** | **278/432** | — |
| side-chain SASA | 47–103 Å² | 38–96 Å² | **7.4 Å²** |
| carboxylate → Arg114 CZ | 10.1–13.9 Å | 9.8–13.5 Å | **3.64 Å** |

The clash filter is doing real work: chain L's closest approach bottoms out at 1.68 Å across the grid, so genuine collisions were rejected. Chain C bottoms out at 2.96 Å — that backbone has unobstructed side-chain space.

The rotamer freedom is large but **the outcome is not ambiguous**: every sterically viable rotamer leaves the carboxylate solvent-exposed at ≥38 Å², five to fourteen times the buried closed-state value, and none brings it within 9.8 Å of Arg114. An Asp on the open backbone can point many ways; none of them reach the groove.

*Caveats.* The virtual Cβ is not inserted into the structure, so it does not occlude its own carboxylate — exposure is a slight over-estimate. Intra-residue contacts at position 6 are excluded from clash detection, so a strained χ1 is not penalised by sterics alone; relaxation handles that. And this is a **wild-type** backbone: 7OW4 has no density at 5–6 in any copy, so the real G12D free peptide is more mobile than any single conformer implies.

### Backbone compatibility

φ/ψ at position 6: 7OW3 C = −69.6°/+48.3°, 7OW3 L = −86.4°/+41.7°, 7OW6 = −37.2°/+117.7°. Both free copies have **negative φ**, so the open backbone is not glycine-exclusive (Gly-only territory is overwhelmingly positive φ) and an Asp can occupy it. The free copies sit in the bridge region between αR and β — allowed but not most-favoured, so relaxation is warranted. The *observed* Asp6 in 7OW6 is also in a sparsely populated φ/ψ combination, so marginal dihedrals at position 6 are a property of this peptide in this groove, not an artefact of a model.

### Affinity calibration (Poole et al. Tables 1–2)

The parent JDI TCR binds G12D at **63 µM** with **no detectable binding** to wild-type, and is clean against G12C, ERAS, RRAS, MRAS, DIRAS2 and RAP1A. As affinity was matured, wild-type binding *appeared*: NB → 25.8 µM → 3.0 µM → 425 nM. **Micromolar affinity is the target, not nanomolar** — over-optimising affinity degrades the objective ranked first.

# Route A — conformational selection on the open state

## Rationale

Design a binder that engages an Asp6 projecting out of the groove, on the experimentally observed **open** backbone of the free complex.

The mechanistic claim is conformational selection, not induced fit: the open state need only be *populated* in the free ensemble, not dominant. The penalty on apparent affinity is −RT ln *p* — about 1.4 kcal/mol at 10% population, 2.7 at 1%. A binder that engages the open state shifts the population toward it.

Four things support this:

1. **The disorder in 7OW4 is evidence for the premise.** No density at residues 5–6 in any of four copies means the free G12D peptide centre is genuinely sampling multiple conformations. Conformational selection requires a populated alternative state; disorder is what that looks like in a diffraction experiment.
2. **The open state is an experimental observation, not a model.** Two independent copies of 7OW3 agree to within 0.5 Å, and the 7OW5 control shows the open/closed flip is driven by receptor binding rather than by the mutation.
3. **Reachability is measured, not inferred.** Every clash-free Asp rotamer on that backbone is solvent-exposed at ≥38 Å².
4. **The discriminator is Asp-versus-nothing, and negative design comes built in.** A pocket presenting a cation and backbone donors sized for a carboxylate is, against wild-type, a desolvated unsatisfied cation — actively destabilising, not merely neutral. The same feature serves objectives 1 and 3. Objective 2 follows: apo HLA has an empty groove and nothing for the reader to grab.

**Known headwinds.** The groove rewards burial — only the G12D peptide forms a network with the HLA F-pocket in the bound state, so the closed conformation is *more* stabilised for the mutant than for WT. And the entropy is paid twice: ordering a mobile region, in a non-preferred conformation. The TCR paid TΔS° = −8.13 kcal/mol across ~990 Å² buried; a 60–70 residue miniprotein buries ~800–900 Å², so there is margin but little.

**Expected outcome:** a binder with a real mechanism and a genuine chance at objective 1. Not a high-probability binder.

**03 — Build the design targets.** 7OW3 copies **C** (HLA chain A) and **L** (chain J), each as peptide + heavy chain + its paired β2m. Mutate Gly6→Asp. Build both copies rather than choosing: chain L has the more comfortable φ and lower B-factors, chain C has unobstructed rotamer space, neither dominates, and they are independent observations of the same conformation. Take 2–3 distinct χ1 wells per copy from the stage-02 scan, preferring high-SASA clash-free rotamers in canonical wells.

**04 — Protonate and relax.** PDBFixer adds hydrogens at pH 7.4 without building
unobserved residues; OpenMM minimises with amber14-all + implicit GBn2 and backbone
heavy atoms harmonically restrained at 10 kcal/mol/Å², every side chain free. All
nine structures — five mutants, two WT and two apo references — get an identical
protocol, so the references remain valid comparators for stage 07.

Result: backbone RMSD 0.097–0.102 Å throughout; every rotamer stayed in its well
(largest χ1 shift 17°); carboxylate SASA 53–82 Å², still far above the closed-state
7.4 Å²; carboxylate-to-Arg114 unchanged at 10.9–13.4 Å. Arg114 side-chain
displacement is 0.236–0.279 Å in the mutants against 0.247–0.271 Å in the references
— indistinguishable, so the groove does not reach for the open aspartate.

**This stage runs no dynamics.** See *Stated shortcomings*.

**05 — Generate backbones.** RFdiffusion binder mode against

recorded as a deviation.

*Design gate.* Applied locally, after generation. A backbone passes when all four
hold:

1. its backbone comes no closer than **2.0 Å** to the target backbone;
2. **Asp6 is an interface residue** — support, rim or core under the Levy
   classification;
3. **Asp6 loses ≥10 Å² of SASA** on binding; and
4. the **peptide carries ≥10%** of the target-side interface ΔSASA.

Criteria 3 and 4 encode the project's two leading objectives separately, and in the
right order. Criterion 3 is absolute and stands for *mutation specificity*: does the
binder actually read Asp6? Criterion 4 is a ratio and stands for *peptide-dependent
binding*: is the peptide central to the interface or incidental to an HLA-dominated
one? Its bar is deliberately the weaker of the two, because specificity outranks
peptide-dependence in this project's ranking.

The gate originally used criterion 4 alone at ≥20%, which inverted that ranking. Over
the first 50 designs it rejected `nim3_8` (41 Å² of Asp6 burial, 17% share) while
admitting `nim1_1` (15 Å², 37% share) and `nim2_4` (6.7 Å², 41% share) — i.e. it
preferred a design that sits on the peptide without touching the mutated residue to
one that buries it. Nine excluded designs had more peptide contact than the weakest
admitted one. The 10 Å² threshold is set at a gap in the observed distribution:
losses run …15.7, 14.7, 11.2, then 6.7, 4.3, 0.

*Stated shortcoming — the gate partly measures a modelling choice.* Asp6 does not
exist in any crystal structure of this complex at the conformer used here: 7OW3
carries wild-type Gly at peptide position 6, so stage 02 scanned the Asp rotamers,
stage 03 built χ1 = −70°, χ2 = +120°, and stage 04 minimised it. Criterion 3 above is
therefore measured against a side chain this project constructed.

`scripts/05c_rotamer_sensitivity.py` quantifies the dependence by re-scoring all 50
designs against an independently built and relaxed alternative (χ1 = −180°, χ2 = 0°),
changing nothing else. **Nine of 50 verdicts move** — three lost, six gained — and
individual swings are large: `nim1_6` goes from 36.8 Å² of Asp6 burial to 4.0 Å², and
`nim3_19` from 0.8 Å² to 21.5 Å². Seventeen designs pass under both rotamers; the
canonical set is 20 and the alternative 23. Full table in
`results/rotamer_sensitivity.tsv`.

Selection uses the canonical rotamer, deliberately and not because the alternative was
unavailable. The consequence is stated rather than engineered away: for designs whose
Asp6 burial is near the 10 Å² threshold, the reported engagement is a property of the
modelled rotamer as much as of the design. Two designs are exceptions and are the more
credible for it — `nim3_6` classifies as `core` under both rotamers (75.8 and 74.5 Å²),
and `nim2_5` is `rim` canonically but `core` under the alternative (52.6 → 61.4 Å²).

A failure mode was predicted before generation and is confirmed: the binder tends to
land on the α1/α2 helix tops, a far larger hydrophobic surface than a ten-residue
peptide in a groove. 18 of 50 designs leave Asp6 fully `surface`. Remedies available
but not needed at this scale: widen hotspots to `[C4,C5,C6,C7,C8]`; or the
`Complex_beta` checkpoint for less helical topologies. Note that *all* 50 designs
came back 84–97% helix with zero strand content, so the topology search never
explored sheets.

Hard validation, separate from the gate because these are bugs rather than design
failures: target backbone RMSD < 0.1 Å measured **without superposition** (RFdiffusion
holds the target fixed, so anything else means a misparse or a wrong contig), binder
length within 60–75, no binder-to-target approach below 2.0 Å, and no one-sided
interface. That last check earns its place — occlusion is mutual, so an interface
appearing on only one side means the generated chain is interpenetrating the target
rather than docked against it. A clash test against the Asp6 carboxylate alone does
*not* catch this: a chain can sit a comfortable distance from the aspartate while
overlapping the HLA elsewhere.

**06 — Design sequences.** ProteinMPNN, `--sampling_temp 0.1 --omit_AAs C`, 8 sequences per backbone.

**07 — Mechanism audit (the primary filter).** Confirm the designed reader group is classified `core` or `support` against the Asp6 carboxylate under the Levy classification above (the 3.2 Å distance criterion this stage previously specified is superseded); then thread the WT peptide into the same model and confirm the reader is left unsatisfied and desolvated. A design that passes has a mechanism; one that fails has a correlation. Cheap, local, and it tests the thing being claimed. Both `biotite.structure.sasa` and freesasa are available, so burial can be cross-checked against an independent implementation.

**08–11** as listed: PRODIGY on G12D and WT-threaded complexes ranked by difference; co-fold finalists against all three targets ranked by ipSAE(G12D) − max(ipSAE(WT), ipSAE(apo)); Foldseek/MMseqs2 novelty; pLDDT, ipSAE and mean Kyte–Doolittle hydropathy.

**Route-specific risk — read before trusting stage 09.** Co-folding models are trained on the PDB, where this peptide's centre is either absent or closed. They may refuse to reproduce the open state and return poor ipSAE for reasons unrelated to design quality. The selection prompt computes ipSAE itself at fixed settings, so the screening metric is structurally biased against this mechanism. Mitigation: state it explicitly in the methods; rely on stage 07 as primary evidence; place the best-argued design in row 1, which the prompt protects (*"a failed screen … never removes a participant's ranked top pick"*).

## Interface definition — Levy rSASA classification

Interface residues are identified by the classification of Levy, *J Mol Biol*
403:660 (2010), everywhere in this project. Distance cutoffs are not used to define
interfaces; closest-approach distances are reported as descriptive numbers only.

Per-residue SASA is computed in the complex and again with the partner group's atoms
removed at identical coordinates, then divided by the theoretical maximum ASA of
Tien et al., *PLoS ONE* 8:e80635 (2013). A residue counts as changed on binding if its
absolute SASA loss exceeds 0.1 Å² — a tolerance rather than `> 0`, because
Shrake–Rupley is a numerical quadrature and its noise must not manufacture interface
residues. At a relative-SASA threshold of 0.25 each residue becomes `interior`,
`surface`, `support`, `rim` or `core`; **interface = support + rim + core**, and
**buried interface = core + support**.

Why this rather than a cutoff: a cutoff counts residues that are near but not
occluded, and says nothing about burial. The classification separates `core` (buried
on binding) from `rim` (at the edge, still solvated), which is precisely the
distinction this project's mechanistic claim rests on — that a designed reader group
is buried and desolvated against the aspartate, not merely nearby.

The implementation (`src/kras_pmhc/interface.py`) takes two *groups* of chains rather
than two chains. The literal per-chain reading counts every internal interface, so on
a binder + HLA + peptide system the HLA–peptide interface would swamp the signal being
measured. Single-chain groups reproduce the per-chain case exactly.

**Validation against a known answer.** On 7OW6, classifying the peptide against the
HLA heavy chain puts Asp6 in `core` — rSASA 0.57 → 0.09, 93 Å² lost — which agrees
with the independent stage-01 measurement of 7.4 Å² side-chain SASA for that residue.
Classifying the same peptide against the TCR instead puts Asp6 in `rim`, losing only
16 Å². So the TCR reads the aspartate without desolvating it, which is Poole et al.'s
indirect-electrostatic mechanism recovered by a method that uses no distances at all.
Both results are asserted in `tests/test_interface.py`.

## Affinity prediction — PRODIGY

Xue et al., *Bioinformatics* 2016, [10.1093/bioinformatics/btw514](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btw514). Installed as `prodigy-prot`.

PRODIGY predicts ΔG from counts of interfacial contacts by residue class plus non-interacting surface. One consequence for the methods document:

- **It suits Route A.** Replacing Asp6 with Gly removes side-chain contacts outright, which PRODIGY registers directly.
Trained on natural complexes with ~1.5 kcal/mol error, so use it for **ranking within a congeneric series**, never as an absolute ΔG.

## Stated shortcomings

These belong in the submitted methods document verbatim. They are deliberate
trades made under a deadline, not oversights.

**1. The population of the open conformer is not measured.** This design rests on
the open conformer being populated enough in the free G12D ensemble for
conformational selection to operate. No molecular dynamics or free-energy
calculation was run to test that — a deliberate trade for submission speed. The
structural evidence establishes only that the open conformer is crystallographically
observed in the wild-type free complex in two independent copies, that an aspartate
is sterically compatible with that backbone, and that every sterically viable
rotamer is solvent-exposed. Whether it is populated enough in solution for a binder
to select it is untested, and is the principal risk to this submission.

Two pieces of evidence bear on it without new compute, and they cut in opposite
directions. In favour: 7OW4, the free G12D structure, has no density at peptide
positions 5–6 in any of four copies, so that region demonstrably samples multiple
conformations — conformational selection needs a populated alternative state, and
disorder is what that looks like in a diffraction experiment. Against: the same
mobility means the single modelled conformer is not well determined. Position-6
B-factors in the two source copies are 48.9 and 57.9 Å² against a structure mean of
37.9 Å².

**2. The open backbone is wild-type.** 7OW3 carries Gly6. The mutant's own free
structure resolves nothing at position 6, so the G12D open backbone is inferred from
the wild-type one. 7OW4's disorder is worse than 7OW3's, suggesting the mutant
peptide is if anything more mobile there.

**3. Relaxation is local, not exhaustive.** Stage 04 minimises under a backbone
restraint. It shows each built rotamer sits at a local minimum and that the HLA
groove does not reach toward the aspartate, but a local minimum is not evidence of
thermodynamic preference.

**4. Hydrogen placement is not deterministic.** PDBFixer's initial hydrogen
positions vary between runs, so stage 04's pre-minimisation energies are not
reproducible run to run (observed spread of several thousand kcal/mol). The
minimised energies are stable to a few kcal/mol out of ~19,800, and all reported
geometry is measured after minimisation.

**5. Only one route was pursued.** An alternative design against the TCR-bound
closed conformation, reproducing the indirect-electrostatic mechanism of Poole et
al., was specified and deliberately not run. Time was spent on depth in one
mechanism rather than breadth across two.
